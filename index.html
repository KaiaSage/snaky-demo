      How Black thinks

      The paper proves the result with cards. A card is a promise of the form: if Black already owns the required cells A and White has no stone in the envelope T, Black wins within h more moves. Cards 0–5 are the six ways to be one stone from Snaky. Every later card plays one pivot stone and holds a hand of child cards whose envelopes have nothing in common except cells Black already owns. So whatever White replies, at least one child card survives, and its height is smaller.

      The final card, 727, needs nothing (A = ∅), fits in a 17×17 square (|T| = 251), and has height 21. Its pivot is the paper's (8,8), which on this board is tengen, the centre point K10. In glass-box mode, every hot cell on the board is a reply that kills the fastest cards in Black's hand.

      The squeeze

      Card 727 is a hand of 32 placed cards in six groups (Table 1 of the paper). Step through them and watch their common envelope shrink until only the pivot is left. That is why Black needs nothing to start.
